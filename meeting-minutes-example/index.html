Meeting minutes example: a township board draft, section by section

By Matt Kerr, who makes Mind the Minutes in Grand Rapids · Checked 9 November 2026

This is a complete example of board meeting minutes. It covers an 8-minute township board meeting with 5 members, 3 motions and one roll-call vote. Each part of the minutes is shown below, followed by what it records and why it is there.

This is a made-up meeting. The board, the people and the votes are invented. The draft came from running Mind the Minutes's transcript tool on its own test meeting on 9 November 2026. It is shown exactly as the tool wrote it, before a clerk checked it.

Good minutes answer four questions: when and where the board met, who was there, what it decided, and how each member voted on any roll call. For Michigan public bodies, the Open Meetings Act lists these in MCL 15.269(1). HOA, church and nonprofit boards are not public bodies under that Act, so check your bylaws for what your minutes must show.

1. The draft label

Draft meeting minutes

Draft for the clerk to correct and the board to approve.

Why it's there: minutes are not final until the board approves them, usually at its next meeting. Michigan's Act treats the two versions separately. Proposed minutes must be available within 8 business days after the meeting, and approved minutes within 5 business days after the meeting that approves them (MCL 15.269(3)). The label tells a reader which version they are holding. When the board approves the minutes, replace it with the approval date.

2. Meeting details

Meeting details

Example Township Board

Meeting type: regular

Date: Saturday, November 7, 2026

Place: Town hall

Start time from meeting details: 7:00 p.m.

Template: Michigan township

Why it's there: date, time and place are the first three items on the list in MCL 15.269(1). "Regular" tells a reader this was a scheduled meeting and not a special one.

What a clerk would fix: "Town hall" is too thin for an official record. Write the building name and street address. The last line only says which format the tool used, and you can delete it.

3. Roll call

Roll call

Present at start: Alex Green, Supervisor; Casey Brown, Clerk; Morgan Lee, Treasurer; Taylor Smith, Trustee.

Absent at start: Jordan Davis, Trustee.

Late arrival: Jordan Davis, 7:04 p.m.

Public present: 3.

Why it's there: members present and members absent are both on the MCL 15.269(1) list. The late arrival line matters here. Trustee Davis came in at 7:04 p.m. and then voted on Motion 2. Without that line, the minutes would show an absent member casting a vote. Titles next to names help a reader match each roll-call vote to a seat. The public count is optional, as the Act does not ask for it.

4. Meeting business

Meeting business

Meeting called to order. Time: 7:00 p.m.

The prior minutes are approved by consent.

Public comment: 2 speakers. Topics: parks, roads.

Treasurer report: the bills are ready for review.

Meeting adjourned. Time: 7:08 p.m.

Why it's there: this is the running record, in order. The call to order and adjournment times show when the meeting began and ended. Approving the last meeting's minutes is a decision, so it gets a line. Had the board corrected those minutes, the Act says the corrected minutes must show both the original entry and the correction (MCL 15.269(1)).

Public comment is not on the Act's list. This draft gives the number of speakers and their topics, and it quotes nobody. One made-up resident said "My exact words should not be copied", and the draft did not copy them. If your board records comments in more detail, add them.

5. Motions and votes

Motions and votes

Motion 1

Motion to approve the agenda.

Moved by: Casey Brown.

Seconded by: Morgan Lee.

Voice vote: passed.

Motion 2

Motion to approve the road repair contract.

Moved by: Taylor Smith.

Seconded by: Casey Brown.

Roll-call vote: passed. Alex Green: yes; Casey Brown: yes; Morgan Lee: abstain; Taylor Smith: yes; Jordan Davis: no.

Motion 3

Motion to buy a new mower.

Moved by: Morgan Lee.

Seconded by: Taylor Smith.

Voice vote: failed.

Why it's there: decisions are the heart of the record, and the Act adds that "the minutes shall include all roll call votes taken at the meeting" (MCL 15.269(1)). Every motion follows the same five lines: what was moved, who moved it, who seconded it, the kind of vote and the result. Keeping that order makes a year of minutes easy to scan.

Motion 2 was a roll call, so each member's vote is listed by name: 3 yes, 1 no and 1 abstain. The draft does not write the count. You may want to add it, for example "carried 3 to 1, with 1 abstaining". The free Michigan template has a line for it.

Motion 3 shows why a clerk reads every result. In the transcript the board says "Aye", and then the chair says the motion fails. The tool writes only the result someone announced, and it never works one out from the voices. So the draft says failed, and the clerk should check the recording before the board approves it.

6. Items to check

Items to check

No rule checks were raised. The clerk must still review the full draft.

Why it's there: when the tool is unsure, it writes [CHECK] in the draft and lists the item here. A missing seconder or a time nobody said aloud both get one. This meeting raised none, but that does not make the draft final. Motion 3 and the address still need a person.

7. The posting reminder

Proposed minutes must be available within 8 business days after the meeting. Approved minutes must be available within 5 business days after the meeting at which they are approved. Checked against MCL 15.269(3) on legislature.mi.gov, 8 November 2026. https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-15-269

Why it's there: it keeps the deadline in front of the clerk. You would delete it before posting. For this meeting on Saturday, 7 November 2026, the safe date for proposed minutes is Wednesday, 18 November 2026. That date counts every Monday to Friday and skips no holidays. If the office was closed for Veterans Day on 11 November, the date may move to Thursday, 19 November. If the board approves the minutes at a meeting on Saturday, 5 December 2026, the safe date for the approved minutes is Friday, 11 December 2026.

What this example leaves out

No closed session was held. When one is, the open minutes show the roll-call vote that called it and its purpose (MCL 15.267(1)). The clerk keeps a separate set of closed-session minutes, which are not public (MCL 15.267(2)).

There is no signature line yet. The clerk adds one, with the date the minutes were submitted. The free Michigan template has the signature, closed session and posting date lines ready to fill in.
